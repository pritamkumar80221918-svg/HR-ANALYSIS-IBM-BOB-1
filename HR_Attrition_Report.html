Business Insights & HR Recommendations

Five data-backed findings with direct, actionable business solutions

🔥 Finding #1 — Critical

Overtime Is the Single Biggest Driver of Attrition

Employees required to work overtime leave at 30.5% — nearly 3× the 10.4% rate of non-overtime employees. With 416 overtime workers (28% of the workforce), this is a systemic risk. The 127 attrited overtime employees each represent recruitment, onboarding, and lost productivity costs estimated at 50–200% of annual salary.

⚠️ Action: Audit all overtime-requiring roles. Cap OverTime hours. Introduce compensatory time-off. Hire additional headcount in Sales and Lab Tech roles to eliminate structural overtime dependency.
📉 Finding #2 — High Priority

Sales Department and Sales Representatives at Critical Risk

Sales has the highest departmental attrition at 20.6%. Sales Representatives specifically show an alarming 39.8% attrition rate — nearly 2 in 5 leave. Attrited employees earn on average $4,787/month, 30% less than retained staff ($6,833), pointing to clear compensation misalignment in junior Sales roles.

⚠️ Action: Benchmark Sales Rep compensation against industry standards. Introduce structured Sales career ladders (Rep → Senior Rep → Executive). Implement 90-day onboarding check-ins and mentorship pairing.
👤 Finding #3 — Early-Career Crisis

Young Employees (18–25) Are Leaving at 35.8% — 3.9× the 36–45 Rate

The 18–25 cohort shows a 35.8% attrition rate — the highest of any age group. Employees in their first 2 years also show 29.8% attrition. Together, these two signals confirm a broken early-career experience: inadequate onboarding, unclear growth paths, and competitive job market alternatives for junior talent.

⚠️ Action: Launch a structured Junior Talent Programme — 18-month rotation, assigned mentor, 6-month stay interview, and performance-linked early bonus. Target retention before the 24-month cliff.
🕑 Finding #4 — Measurable & Preventable

Low Job Satisfaction Doubles Attrition; Poor Work-Life Balance Triples It

Employees at Job Satisfaction Level 1 leave at 22.8% vs 11.3% at Level 4 — a 2× gap. Work-Life Balance Level 1 employees leave at 31.2% vs 14.2% at Level 3 — a 2.2× gap. These are leading indicators HR can act on before resignation, through regular pulse surveys and manager alerts.

⚠️ Action: Deploy quarterly engagement pulse surveys. Auto-flag any employee with Satisfaction ≤ 2 for a manager 1-on-1 within 2 weeks. Introduce flexible working for all employees scoring WLB ≤ 2.
🏠 Finding #5 — Hidden Retention Levers

Commute Distance and Frequent Business Travel Are Silent Attrition Drivers

Employees living 16+ km from the office leave at 20.7% — 50% more than those near the office (13.8%). Frequent business travellers show 24.9% attrition vs just 8.0% for non-travellers. These lifestyle stressors compound with overtime and low satisfaction to push employees over the resignation threshold.

⚠️ Action: Offer hybrid/remote work for employees 16+ km away. Review travel frequency caps in high-attrition roles. Introduce travel recovery days (1 day off per 3-day trip) and a travel hardship allowance.
💍 Bonus Finding

Single Employees Leave at 2.5× the Rate of Divorced Employees

Single employees show a 25.5% attrition rate vs 10.1% for divorced and 12.5% for married. This correlates strongly with the younger age profile of single employees and their higher career mobility. Without a strong social/community anchor at work, single junior employees are the easiest to lose to competitors.

⚠️ Action: Build team belonging through ERGs (Employee Resource Groups), social events, and team-based incentives. Communicate transparent promotion timelines to all employees under 30.

Executive Summary

For senior management review — key conclusions and recommended priorities

IBM's workforce of 1,470 employees is experiencing an overall attrition rate of 16.1% (237 employees) — above the industry benchmark of 10–12%. The financial cost is material: attrited employees earn on average $4,787/month vs $6,833 for retained staff, indicating that the company is disproportionately losing lower-compensated junior employees. Assuming replacement cost of 50% of annual salary per employee, total attrition cost is estimated at ~$6.8 million per year.

Analysis across 35 workforce dimensions reveals five compounding root causes: (1) mandatory overtime — with a 30.5% attrition rate for affected employees; (2) compensation misalignment in Sales Representative roles — at 39.8% attrition; (3) early-career disengagement in the 18–25 and 0–2 year tenure cohorts — at 35.8% and 29.8% respectively; (4) low job satisfaction and poor work-life balance — both directly doubling or tripling attrition probability; and (5) commute distance and frequent travel — adding lifestyle friction that pushes already-at-risk employees to resign.

Three immediate priorities recommended for the next 90 days: First, conduct a full overtime audit across Sales and R&D and enforce a maximum weekly overtime cap. Second, launch a compensation benchmarking review for Sales Representatives and Laboratory Technicians against market rates. Third, deploy an automated engagement score system that flags employees with satisfaction ≤ 2 or WLB ≤ 2 for proactive manager outreach. These three actions alone target over 60% of all attrition risk identified in this analysis.
